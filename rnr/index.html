A Men's Convent in a building that needs saving.

Across Saskatchewan and beyond, historic buildings stand empty. RnR is a way to bring them back: a community of men restores the building and learns a trade doing it.

Why it matters

Restoring a building is the work. Behind it are five problems that hit men hardest, and RnR takes them on together.

9,000

The trades are retiring

Saskatchewan's construction industry expects about 9,000 workers to retire by 2034, nearly a quarter of its workforce. Without new tradesmen, keeping our buildings and infrastructure running only gets more expensive.

17.6

Men are dying by suicide

Saskatchewan's suicide rate is 17.6 per 100,000 people, against 10.9 across Canada. Nationally, men account for nearly 75% of suicide deaths.

1,499

Homelessness is rising

Saskatoon's 2024 count found at least 1,499 people experiencing homelessness, nearly triple the 2022 count. Half of them were men.

49%

Young men are checking out

Nearly half of Canadian men in their twenties live with their parents, more than women. After 30 years of "get a degree," only about 1 in 4 young men has one, against 2 in 5 young women. A trade, a crew and a purpose give them a way back in.

1 in 3

Addiction is hitting the trades

About 1 in 3 Canadian construction workers in safety-sensitive jobs say they've used alcohol or drugs just before or during work, or worked hungover or high. Of Canadians who died of an overdose while employed, 30 to 50% worked in the trades.

A Men's Convent meets all five at once. Men learn a trade by saving a building no one else will, in a substance-free community that feeds them, works beside them and gives them a purpose.

The idea

Walk in homeless, walk out with purpose. Restoring the building is itself the program.

Restoration is the program

Men restore the building in small teams led by certified journeymen: floors, woodwork, windows, plaster and rooms. Each man leaves with a documented trade portfolio.

Bottom-up, not top-down

There's no fixed master plan. Problems are logged, and the expert in charge of each trade area chooses the projects and the crews, within a heritage conservation plan.

Journeymen are honoured guests

Skilled tradespeople volunteer their time. They can come and stay for up to a week to guide the younger men and pass their trade on.

Everyone contributes

Disciplined work toward one shared purpose. Anyone can learn any trade or skill. Men who are sick or injured are looked after first.

Christ-centred, open to all

Led by Jesus and the Holy Spirit. Men of any faith or none are welcome. Worship is offered, never required.

Non-profit and open

No profiteering. The work is recorded and documented openly, so the community can see the building come back to life.

About five years, three phases

Each building opens a little more to the public as the work goes on.

Phase 1

Rescue

A men's convent moves in. Secure the site, deal with emergencies like water, clean out and stabilize.

Public use: about 1%
Phase 2

Restoration

Teams restore the building room by room. A recovery program begins.

Public use: about 5%
Phase 3

Freedom Learning Hub

The restored building opens most of its space to the community: a place where people of all ages learn, research and build.

Public use: over half

Kitchen Boss wanted

Food is the first service. Before the beds are built and before the first wall is repaired, every man on site gets fed. We're looking for the man who will run that kitchen.

Feed the crew

Feed up to 50 men three meals a day, mostly from donated food. There's one dish on the menu each day: the Surprise of the Day.

Teach as you cook

Every man in the kitchen is learning. You train them in cooking, food safety and running a kitchen, and you sign off the skills they earn.

Lead the first trade area

The kitchen opens first. As Kitchen Boss you choose the projects and the crews, and you oversee the journeymen who come to help.

A go-getter in the kitchen

You love running a kitchen, and you've fed a crowd on a tight budget at a camp, a work camp, a mission or a busy restaurant.

Of one mind

You follow Christ and share the convent's faith and purpose. You serve men of any faith or none with the same care.

Volunteer first, needs met

We keep salaries as low as possible. If you can afford to volunteer, that's the goal. If you need money, we'll find it for what you need beyond your basics. The more effort you put in, the more resources you get.
